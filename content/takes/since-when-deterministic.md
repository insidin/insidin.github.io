Organizations already run on decisions that are not predictable or reproducible: human judgment, risk assessments, forecasts, statistical models. We manage them through understanding, assessment and controls. AI deserves the same discipline, not a veto for being probabilistic.

The real question is the difference between determining what should happen and determining what is allowed to happen. An AI system can evaluate, propose or even choose an action. That does not give it the right to define its own decision rights. Those boundaries must be explicit, and enforced by the architecture, not left to a model's instructions. That is the work I focus on when designing AI architectures, platforms and governance models.

Uncertainty is not something to eliminate everywhere. The task is to decide where it is acceptable, where it is not, and to measure how the system actually behaves. I unpack this in an article, including why Jev-style decision models are a useful building block here.

So much content is created and shared today with very little in it. Well structured, polished, headings and bullets in all the right places, and you finish it wondering: what exactly was the message? It even has a name now: workslop.

**The problem is not that AI was used. It is that as a reader, you cannot tell what stands behind it.** Did someone read all of it? Check what matters? Or was it generated, formatted and sent? That one answer decides how much attention and confidence I should give it. Today I can only guess, and every reader has to make that guess again, every time.

There is an uncomfortable part. Several studies suggest readers trust content less once they learn AI was involved, even when a human reviewed it. That rewards staying quiet. But staying quiet does not make the content better, it just moves the judgment to the reader. And that is exactly what we complain about when we receive AI slop.

To those of us who advise, architect and write for a living: our content is what clients rely on. If you use AI to create it, how do you let your readers assess how far they can trust it? Not a disclaimer in a footer that nobody reads, but something that tells them what they can rely on.

**That is why I created a simple scale for it: one part for how AI contributed to making the content, one part for how far a human checked the result. I call it the AI Made/Checked Scale, AI MC for short.** Yes, MC is also the master of ceremonies, and that is intended: someone should still be running the show. I will start applying it to my own content.

Architects, consultants, analysts: would you put such a label on your own deliverables? And would your clients want it? Who is the MC of your content: you or the AI?
